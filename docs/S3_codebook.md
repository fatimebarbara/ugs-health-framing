# S3.  Codebook: full definitions, examples, procedure and proposed changes

*Supplementary material to: Hegyi, F.B. What Is a Park For? Measuring the Health Framing of Urban Green Space Across 22 European Capitals.*

This file complements Appendix C of the paper, which gives the accept/reject rule, three decision rules for hard cases, the six functions with one example each, and the two main boundaries. It adds what the coder saw, the keys, the reject reasons as entered, a fourth decision rule, the full definition of each function with further examples and exclusions, all boundary cases, the procedure, and changes proposed for a second version.

## S3.1  Status of this codebook

The codebook was not written in full before the census began. The categories, the keys and the six reject reasons were fixed in the coding script before the first post was coded; the decision rules below were applied during coding and are written down here as they were applied. Where a rule was formalised only during or after coding, this is stated. The lists of indicators under each function in S3.4 were compiled for this codebook from the coded posts: they describe how the coding was done and are checked against the examples given, but they are not a protocol the coder had in front of her during the census. This matters for how the reliability results in §4.4 of the paper are read: part of the drift they record is the rules becoming explicit.

## S3.2  What the coder saw, and the keys

The unit is one post, read on its own. Threads were not joined and replies were read without the post they answer.

Posts were presented one at a time in the R console, in census row order, with four pieces of information: the city, the retrieval stem that brought the post in (green, park, or both), the date, and the text (Figure 1 of the paper). The two automated tier columns were removed from the working file before coding began, so the coder could not see whether the lexicon had flagged a post. The console shows text only; images and videos attached to a post were not visible.

The keys were:

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| h | health and wellbeing |
| c | climate and environment |
| a | amenity and civic pride |
| e | equity and access |
| $ | economic |
| n | ceremonial or none |
| r | reject, followed by a number for the reason |
| s | skip; the post stays uncoded and returns in a later session |
| q | quit and save |

Several function keys can be typed together (hca, ae). After each post the coder could type a free-text note; the file was saved after every post. No post remained uncoded at the end of the census.

## S3.3  Step 1 in detail

Accept a post when it refers to a physical green space or one of its elements. A green space is publicly accessible land within the city that is vegetated: a park, a garden, a forest or wood, trees, a lawn or meadow, a green corridor or greenway, a green roof, a riverbank or waterfront with planting. Private gardens are excluded, because the public health argument concerns places people can use.

The reference can be made in four ways, and each counts:

1. A common noun for green space in any language of the dataset: park, garden, tree, forest, zieleń, verde pubblico.
2. A locally specific term: the Roman villa, the Catalan superilla, the Viennese Coole Straße.
3. A named place used as a place: a post about planting trees in Phoenix Park refers to a green space; a post giving Phoenix Park as the address of a press conference does not (S2, class 5).
4. An act of greening: planting trees, depaving a square, adding greenery to a street.

Public spaces that are often but not always green — a square, a promenade, a playground, a sports ground — are accepted when the post presents them as green or as being greened, or when they are a recognised green public space of the city. A square named only as the location of an event is not a green space reference.

The adjective green on its own is not a reference. Green is accepted only when it describes a physical place or the city's vegetation ("more greenery", "a greener street"), and rejected when it describes a party, a policy, an economy, a light or a colour.

### Reject reasons as entered

A rejected post is given one of seven reasons, defined in Appendix B and S2.2. The coding script offered six, typed as a number after r:

| Number | Reason |
|---|---|
| 1 | party-political green |
| 2 | no green space referent |
| 3 | proper noun |
| 4 | cross-language false friend |
| 5 | policy metaphor |
| 6 | vehicular park |
| 7 | within-language substring (added by the coder during the census; not in the script's menu) |

Where more than one applies, the reason recorded is the one that explains why the stem matched (S2.1).

### A fourth decision rule

Appendix C gives rules R1 to R3. One further rule was applied:

R4. The visible text decides. When the word that retrieved a post cannot be found in the visible text (for example because it sits inside a link) and nothing in the text refers to a green space, the post is rejected as no referent. Example: Vienna, r1372, a list of funded medical research projects, retrieved by a match the coder could not see and rejected as no referent; the coder's note records "retrieval trigger not visible".

## S3.4  Step 2: full definitions of the six functions

### The general rule

Code every function the author attributes to the green space. A function counts when the post says, in words or by plain implication, that the green space does this, serves this, or is for this. It does not count when the function is mentioned elsewhere in the post without being linked to the green space. The coding is multi-label: a post can carry any combination of the five substantive functions. Ceremonial or none is exclusive: it is used only when none of the other five applies, and in the final census it never occurs with another code.

The functions describe what is said, not what the coder believes green space does. A post about a new park that says nothing about health is not coded health, however strong the epidemiological evidence.

### h — Health and wellbeing

Code h when the post presents the green space as good for physical or mental health or wellbeing, either by naming health directly or by naming an activity or condition through which green space affects health: exercise, sport, walking, running, play, cycling as activity; rest, calm, relief from stress; cleaner air, less noise, shade or cooling for people; social contact presented as good for people.

Examples. Dublin, r546: "We are launching parkHIIT in Poppintree Park tomorrow, book your place for our free workout session 💪" — coded h. Barcelona, r17: "Un gran repte de la #BarcelonaFutura és passar del gris de l'asfalt a la ciutat verda i saludable" [A great challenge for the Barcelona of the future is to go from the grey of asphalt to the green and healthy city] — coded h and a. Barcelona, r34: "la lluita per una Barcelona més verda i menys contaminada és clau per a la salut dels nostres nens i nenes" [the fight for a greener, less polluted Barcelona is key to the health of our children] — coded h and e.

Not h. A mention of hospitals or health services in a post about something else. Cycling or walking presented only as transport, with no suggestion that it is good for the people doing it — this boundary was applied inconsistently (see S3.5, B1).

### c — Climate and environment

Code c when the post presents the green space as serving the climate or the environment: mitigation (trees and carbon), adaptation (cooling the city, holding rainwater, flood protection), biodiversity and wildlife, protection of nature, pollution as an environmental rather than a human problem, and climate action located in a green space.

Examples. Dublin, r536: "Dedicated wildlife crime unit to be established in the National Parks & Wildlife Service" — coded c. Dublin, r444: "I'm attending a @theactionnet event: Albert College Park #ClimateStrike Support Action Sept 20" — coded c.

Not c. The adjective green on its own, or a reference to the European Green Deal with no green space (rejected at step 1, class 3).

### a — Amenity and civic pride

Code a when the post says what people will do in the place or get from it — enjoyment, leisure, beauty, a place to sit, to meet, to have coffee, to hold an event — or presents the green space as something the city can be proud of. Provision counts: a post that lists parks among the things a new district will have is coded a.

Examples. Brussels, r235: "nous avons inauguré le S'Ket Park dans le Bois de la Cambre pour que jeunes et moins jeunes puissent s'y amuser tout l'été!" [we opened the S'Ket Park in the Bois de la Cambre so that young and not-so-young can enjoy themselves there all summer] — coded a and e. Prague, r1015: "Nová čtvrť vznikne u Nákladového nádraží Žižkov. Budou tam parky, školy, nová tramvajová trať, obchody, služby…" [A new district will be built at the Žižkov freight station. There will be parks, schools, a new tram line, shops, services…] — coded a.

The amenity boundary. Amenity requires the post to say what people will do in the place or get from it. A bare announcement or celebration of a place, without that, is ceremonial (n). This rule was written down in the coder's note of 22 September, at the end of the first pass; posts coded before that date were coded without it in written form. It is the boundary with the lowest self-agreement in the re-code (alpha 0.11 on ceremonial or none, §4.4).

### e — Equity and access

Code e when the post says who the green space is for or who can reach it: children, older people, families, people with disabilities, a named neighbourhood that lacked green space; free or open access; accessibility; participation in decisions about the space (a consultation, a vote on a design).

Examples. Barcelona, r82: "Si els parcs són accessibles per la teva filla, seran millors per tots els nens i nenes, seran millors per tothom" [If parks are accessible for your daughter, they will be better for all children, better for everyone] — coded a and e. Dublin, r369: "I'll be speaking in support of College Green Plaza at Board Pleanála Hearing tomorrow. Let's create a child-friendly space in the heart of the city!" — coded e.

### $ — Economic

Code $ when the post presents the green space as an economic matter: jobs, investment, tourism, property values, local business, costs and savings, or the city's green space as an economic resource.

Example. Rome, r1152: "Per governare #Roma ci sono da risolvere emergenze drammatiche, ma anche straordinarie risorse: il verde, la ricchezza dei talenti, università e ricerca, distretti industriali importanti, un patrimonio storico artistico unico al mondo. Valorizziamole!" [To govern Rome there are dramatic emergencies to solve, but also extraordinary resources: its green spaces, its wealth of talent, universities and research, important industrial districts, a historic and artistic heritage unique in the world. Let's make the most of them!] — coded $.

Economic is the rarest function (32 of 779 posts) and the one with the highest self-agreement (alpha 0.88), because its signal is lexically distinct.

### n — Ceremonial or none

Code n when the post refers to a green space but attributes none of the five functions to it. The code is a residual, not a judgement of the post: many such posts are warm and substantive — an opening, a commemoration, thanks to volunteers, an event held in a park.

Examples. Brussels, r204: "«La Halte»: le nouveau Pocket Park à Laeken" [La Halte: the new pocket park in Laeken] — coded n. Warsaw, r1440: "#NaŻywo: uroczystości w Parku Wolności przy Muzeum Powstania Warszawskiego" [Live: ceremonies in Freedom Park at the Warsaw Rising Museum] — coded n.

The category does two jobs: it holds posts that mark a place ceremonially and posts that attribute nothing at all. S3.8 proposes splitting it.

## S3.5  Boundary cases

These are the boundaries where the coder's decisions were least consistent. They are documented so that a second coder, or a second version of this codebook, can decide them in advance.

B1. Health and amenity. Activity in a green space is health when the post presents it as exercise or as good for people, and amenity when it presents it as leisure or enjoyment. Many posts do both. The census contains two Warsaw posts of the same kind, coded differently. r1450, re-coded in the re-screen: "Jest więcej zieleni, wygodne trasy dla rowerów i nowe naziemne przejścia dla pieszych. Jest bezpieczniej, jest bardziej dostępnie i zielono" [There is more greenery, comfortable cycle routes and new pedestrian crossings. It is safer, more accessible and green] — coded h, c, a and e. r1460, first pass: "Przejść Alejami Jerozolimskimi w cieniu drzew, bezpiecznie przejechać rowerem czy usiąść na kawę wśród zieleni" [To walk along Aleje Jerozolimskie in the shade of trees, to cycle safely or sit down for a coffee among the greenery] — coded a and e. A rule that walking, cycling or shade named as something people will do in the green space counts as health would code both h.

Rules B2 and B4 are proposed for version 2 from the pattern of the coded posts; the census coding has not been checked against them.

B2. Health and climate (proposed). Shade, cooling and heat can be framed for people (h) or for the city (c). Code h when the post says people will be cooler, protected or more comfortable; c when it says the city will be cooler or better adapted; both when it says both.

B3. Amenity and ceremonial. See the amenity boundary in S3.4. An opening that says what the place offers is a; an opening that only names the place is n.

B4. Equity and amenity (proposed). "For everyone" alone is amenity; "for everyone, including those who could not use it before" is equity.

B5. Party content. See R1 in Appendix C. When a post with party content was kept, it was coded only for the functions it attributes to the green space, not for the party's wider programme.

## S3.6  Procedure

The coding ran in four stages.

1. Pilot. A stratified sample of 105 posts across all 23 cities was coded before the census. The pilot fixed the six functions, the keys and the first six reject reasons, and supplied most of the new lexicon terms (S1.1, step 3). Its codes were carried into the census as first-pass codes.

2. Census, first pass. All 1,520 posts were coded between 17 August and 22 September 2026, in sessions of varying length, with the working file saved after every post. The first pass accepted 1,278 posts.

3. Re-code for reliability. A sample of 128 posts, drawn with a fixed seed from the 679 coded between 17 August and 1 September, was re-coded blind to the first-pass codes in the fourth week of September (§3.7 of the paper). The re-code was allowed to reject.

4. Re-screen. On 29 September every accepted post in the re-screen list — accepted posts without a Layer A term at word boundaries (S2.4 gives the exact scope) — was read again: 610 posts, of which 499 were rejected with a reason and 111 kept and re-coded from scratch. The final census is 779 valid posts.

## S3.7  What the codebook does not cover

The codebook records what function a post attributes to green space. It does not record the scale the post speaks at — a single tree or street, the city as a whole, or a national, European or global framework — although the coder observed large differences of this kind between accounts (§5.6 of the paper). It does not record tone, the account holder's role at the time of posting, or whether the post was written by the mayor or by staff.

## S3.8  Changes proposed for version 2

1. Split ceremonial or none into two codes: ceremonial (an opening, a commemoration, thanks, a celebration of a place) and none (a green space named, nothing attributed).
2. Write the health/amenity rule (B1) before coding, and decide whether activity named as something people will do in a green space is health in its own right.
3. Add a three-value scale-of-reference code: a specific object or street; the city as a whole; a named national, European or global framework.
4. Record, for each account, the dates of the holder's term of office, and code only posts written during it.
5. Code with two readers on a common sample of at least 200 posts, reconcile the disagreements into this codebook, and only then divide the census.
6. Apply the exclusion stems of Appendix B (Table B3) before coding, so that the coder's attention goes to the decisions only a reader can make.
