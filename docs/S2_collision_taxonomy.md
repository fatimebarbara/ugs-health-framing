# S2.  Collision taxonomy: decision rules, triggers, examples and known inconsistencies

*Supplementary material to: Hegyi, F.B. What Is a Park For? Measuring the Health Framing of Urban Green Space Across 22 European Capitals.*

This file complements Appendix B of the paper, which defines the seven classes and gives their counts by pass and by city (Tables B1 and B2) and the exclusion stems by language (Table B3). It adds, for each class, the full decision rule, the strings that triggered it, further examples and why it concentrates where it does, followed by what each coding pass caught and where the class assignment is inconsistent.

## S2.1  When more than one class applies

Where more than one class applied, the coder assigned the class that explains why the stem matched. Party-political green takes precedence over policy metaphor when a party or its candidates are named; within-language substring takes precedence over no referent when the matched string is a non-green word containing the green stem; proper noun takes precedence over no referent when the match is a name. The rule that distinguishes a city's own greenness from party-political green is given in S2.2, class 1.

## S2.2  The seven classes

### Class 1.  Party-political green

Definition. The green stem names a political party, its members, candidates, coalitions, electoral results or campaign material, in any language. Includes the party sense of green used as an adjective for a politician or a government.

Decision rule. Reject when the post is about the party or its politics. Retain when the post uses green for the city's own character — a Green Capital award, a greener Budapest — and code the function as none unless one is stated. Retain also when a post with party content names a green space and says what it is for, and code it from what it says: the Dublin post shown in Figure 1 of the paper credits the Green Party with funding for walking, cycling and parks, and was kept and coded health, climate and amenity. The test is whether the sentence still makes sense if green is replaced by the party's name: Vote Green, Retweet if you ever voted Green, türkis-grün.

Triggers. green, Greens, Green Party (118 occurrences of green alone); the handles @greenparty_ie, @cbyrnegreen, @cathmartingreen; the hashtags #WantGreenVoteGreen, #GreensInGovernment, #GreenWave, #Green19, #EuropeanGreens, Greens/EFA; German Grüne, Grünen, türkis-grün, rot-grün; Finnish vihreät, vihreiden; Hungarian zöld in party contexts.

Examples. Vienna, r1351: "Ich persönlich bedaure es sehr, dass #türkis-#grün hier nichts zustande bringt." Berlin, r98: "Enteignungen wie Linke und Grüne sie fordern sind kein gutes Zeichen für den Startup- und Wirtschaftsstandort Berlin." Dublin, r442: "Retweet if you ever voted Green." (The Dublin post previously quoted here, r374, on the Greens and farmers, was coded policy metaphor, not party; it is listed under class 3.) Helsinki, r694: a reply thread addressed to @vihreat and other party accounts.

Why it concentrates. The Dublin account belongs to a Green Party politician, and 109 of its 183 rejections are this class. Helsinki, Budapest, Berlin and Vienna have Green parties in government or in contention, and their mayors address them. Where Green parties are marginal — Madrid, Paris, Warsaw, Sofia — the class is almost empty.

### Class 2.  No green space referent

Definition. The stem matched, but nothing in the post denotes a green space: colour uses, a surname, a word fragment in a URL or handle, an unrelated compound.

Decision rule. Reject when no reading of the post places a park, garden, tree or equivalent in it. This is the residual class and is applied after the five more specific classes have been excluded.

Triggers. The most frequent single trigger was the handle @ZelenskyyUa and the name Zelensky, which contain the Slavic green stem zelen (34 rejected posts across twelve cities during 2022, most in Paris, Prague, Vilnius, Helsinki and Riga; two further Brussels posts that mention him were kept, because they concern the Grand-Place). Others: a bridge lit in green for St Patrick's Day; Latvian and Polish compounds that contain the stem by accident; Swedish grundskolan (primary school) where the Danish/German rule in class 4 had not been extended to Swedish.

Examples. Riga, r1099: "No Ukrainas prezidenta Volodimira @ZelenskyyUa izskanēja trīs vienkārši lūgumi Eiropas pilsētu mēriem." Vienna, r1391: "leuchtet auch am heutigen #StPatricksDay traditionell die Donaustadtbrücke in grün." Brussels, r162: a mask mandate "op heel het Brussels grondgebied" (grondgebied, territory).

### Class 3.  Policy metaphor

Definition. Green used in its policy or economic sense — a programme, a fund, a transition, a deal, a light — without a physical green space.

Decision rule. Reject when green modifies a policy object (deal, recovery, economy, finance, energy, transition, jobs, light) and no physical space is named. Retain when the same post also names a park or tree, and code the function from the full post.

Triggers. green (52), Italian verde (34, overwhelmingly economia verde and transizione verde), #GreenNewDeal, #GreenRecovery, #GreenDeal, #GreenDecade, greener, Catalan verda in fiscalitat justa i verda, Polish zielonych in green jobs contexts. Spanish luz verde (green light) belongs here by definition but was coded under class 6 in the Madrid posts where it occurs; see S2.4.

Examples. Barcelona, r9: "No hi ha futur sense horitzó verd. Cal apostar per un #GreenNewDeal europeu." Rome, r1108: a report on "finanza etica e sostenibile." Lisbon, r731: "Taking action for clean energy innovation." Madrid, r808: "Madrid limpio y verde ha sido siempre la seña de identidad de nuestra ciudad" — rejected because no space is named; the city's greenness as an identity claim. Dublin, r374: "It is time to slay the myth that the Greens and farmers are on different sides."

Why it concentrates. Rome's 43 cases are the green economy in the mayor's posts on budgets, recovery funds and the European Green New Deal — economia verde, transizione verde, finanza sostenibile, a budget with un'anima verde; Dublin's 44 are the Green Party's policy vocabulary; Barcelona's 12 are Green New Deal advocacy.

### Class 4.  Within-language substring

Definition. The green stem sits inside an unrelated common word of the same language, so that substring matching fires on ordinary prose.

Decision rule. Reject when the matched string is part of a word whose meaning has nothing to do with green. The class is defined by the language, not the post, and its triggers can be listed exhaustively per language (Table B3 in the paper).

Triggers. German Grund (reason, ground) and its family: Grund, Gründe, gründen, Gründung, gegründet, Grundsatz, grundsätzlich, Grundlage, Grundstein, Grundsteinlegung, Grundversorgung, Grundschule, Grundschulalter, Grundlagenforschung, Grundvoraussetzung, Grundzüge (Vienna 60, Berlin 29). Danish grund in the same senses: grund, grunden, grundene, grundlag, grundvand (groundwater), ingen grund til (Copenhagen 34). Swedish Gröndal, grundskolan (Stockholm 4). Lithuanian žalą (damage) against žalias (green) (Vilnius 1).

Examples. Vienna, r1321: "Funktionierende Grundversorgung ist auch vertrauensbildend." Copenhagen, r287: "ingen grund til at vente, let's do it!" Berlin, r94: "Der Rechtsanspruch für Ganztagsbetreuung an Grundschulen kommt." Stockholm, r1291: a housing ground-breaking "i Gröndal" — a district name, placed here rather than in class 5 because the stem is inside the toponym.

Why it concentrates. Grund is among the commonest nouns in German and Danish, so a German- or Danish-speaking mayor writing ordinary prose produces false matches without mentioning green at all; a Catalan or Spanish mayor does not. This class is the largest single reason why Vienna, Copenhagen and Berlin lose 78 to 93 per cent of retrieved posts, and it is why the per-language rejection rate must be reported.

### Class 5.  Proper noun

Definition. The stem is part of a name — a place, an organisation, a product, a person — used as a name rather than as a green space.

Decision rule. Reject when the match is a name and the post does not use the named thing as a green space. A named park used as a space (we planted trees in Parc de la Ciutadella) is retained; a named park used as an address or a location for something else (a plaque in Sigmund-Freud-Park) is a judgement call and was rejected in the one instance that arose.

Triggers. Greenland (Groenlandia, Grönland); Cabo Verde and @caboverde_gov; Grünheide (the Tesla site near Berlin); Grunewald (a Berlin district and forest; see S2.4); Green Power System Renewables (a company); Bulgarian Зелена as part of an organisation name; Italian Verdi as a surname and in @_verdi; Lithuanian Žaliojo in a street name.

Examples. Lisbon, r728: "Director of Green Power System Renewables." Berlin, r103: "dass Tesla sich in Grünheide ansiedelt."

### Class 6.  Cross-language false friend

Definition. A term from one language's lexicon matches text in another language, or a borrowed term matches in a sense the lexicon did not intend.

Decision rule. Reject when the match is in a language other than the one the term was listed for, or when a borrowed English term carries a non-green sense in the local language.

Triggers. Green Pass (the Italian COVID certificate, 3 posts in Rome); Spanish luz verde (green light; three Madrid posts, see S2.4); Bulgarian troleybusen park (trolleybus fleet; Sofia 5); Czech les (forest) matching the French article; Danish have (garden) matching the English verb; Lithuanian sodas (garden) matching an English plural.

Examples. Rome, r1141: "giusta posizione di Draghi su #Greenpass." Sofia, r1216: "почти 100% от тролейбусния парк ще е нов" — a trolleybus fleet. Madrid, r851: "Luz verde a la licitación del nuevo contrato de limpieza."

### Class 7.  Vehicular park

Definition. Park, parking and their equivalents in the sense of leaving a vehicle.

Decision rule. Reject when park is a verb or denotes a car park, park-and-ride, parking permit or parked vehicles. Retain when a park-and-ride scheme is set against a green space in the same post and the green space carries the argument (Prague, r966, a park proposed on the roof of an underground car park, was rejected because the argument was about the car park).

Triggers. English park, parking, Park 'n' stride; Czech parkoviště; Bulgarian паркирали.

Examples. Dublin, r683: "you can park for free for up to 1 hour in a designated car park." Sofia, r1235: street cleaning "затруднява от паркирали автомобили."

## S2.3  What the two coding passes caught

The first pass rejected 242 posts; the second, applied only to accepted posts without a word-boundary Layer A match, rejected a further 499. The second pass caught 177 of the 204 party-political cases and 134 of the 149 policy metaphors — the two classes that depend on recognising a political vocabulary rather than a word form. It also caught 77 of 129 substrings, because the Danish grund pattern was only recognised as a class after the German one. No vehicular park was caught on the second pass; that class is lexically obvious and was complete from the start.

The practical lesson is that collision classes 1 and 3 are learned from the dataset, not known in advance, and that any coding protocol for this kind of material should build in a second screening after the coder has seen several hundred posts.

## S2.4  Classification notes and known inconsistencies

The classes were applied by a single coder over five weeks and the assignment is not perfectly consistent at the margins. The deposited log keeps the coder's class as assigned; the following are the places a user of the log should know about.

Zelensky. Thirty-three of the 34 rejected posts matched on the Ukrainian president's name and handle were classed as no referent; one as proper noun. They are a proper noun by the definition in S2.2; the coder's choice reflects that the post as a whole has no green content, which is the test applied for class 2. Either class is defensible; the count for class 5 would rise to 81 if they were all moved.

Luz verde. The four Madrid posts with luz verde (green light) were classed as cross-language false friend (three) and proper noun (one). It is a policy metaphor in the local language, not a cross-language collision; class 3 is the better assignment and the counts would move accordingly.

Grunewald. Six Berlin posts matched Grunewald, a district that is also one of Berlin's largest forests. They were classed as proper noun because the posts used it as a district name. A post about the forest as a forest would be retained.

Named parks as locations. Sigmund-Freud-Park (Vienna, r1399) and College Green (Dublin, r343, r435, r437 — the public space in front of Trinity College) were rejected, the first as proper noun and the three Dublin posts as party-political green. Both are real public spaces used in the posts as locations rather than as green space. The decision to reject is the coder's and was kept; a stricter reading of the rule in Appendix B would retain the College Green posts and code the function as none or amenity. Two of the three (r435, r437) describe people walking freely on College Green without traffic, and would on that reading be coded amenity.

A city's own greenness. The rule retains posts such as a greener Budapest and rejects party uses. In practice the line was drawn at whether a physical space was named: Madrid limpio y verde (r808) was rejected as policy metaphor because none was. The rule in the paper should be read with that qualification.

Pass effects. Because classes 1 and 3 were mostly caught on the second pass, their counts are sensitive to how the second pass was scoped. Two consequences follow.

First, the second pass did not reach every accepted post without a Layer A term. By the matching rule of Appendix A, 688 of the 1,278 posts accepted on the first pass contain no Layer A term; the re-screen file holds 610 of them. The other 78 — 77 retrieved on the green stem, 32 of them in Dublin, 16 in Barcelona and 12 in Brussels — kept their first-pass codes. At least two are plainly party content: a Barcelona post on a visit to the Supreme Court with Green members of the European Parliament (r8), and a Dublin reply referring to "my Green colleague" (r451). The difference between 688 and 610 probably reflects a slightly different matching rule when the re-screen list was drawn; it cannot now be reconstructed.

Second, accepted posts that do contain a Layer A term were not re-screened, and a few collisions remain among the 779 valid posts because the collision word is itself a Layer A variant or sits beside one. Examples: a Brussels post announcing "groen licht" (green light) for a merged hospital, matched through the Dutch variant groen (r205); a Barcelona post on parking rules for the zona blava and zona verda, which are parking zones (r48); and a Brussels post on free Park&Ride (r237). A search of the valid posts for party, green-light, parking and green-deal wording returns 43 posts; most of them, like the Figure 1 post, name a green space and were kept on purpose, but a third pass over these 40 would remove the few that do not.

## S2.5  Deposited file

`data/collision_log.csv` — one row per rejected post (741): row (census row identifier), city, account, tweet_id, date, retrieval_stem (green, park or both), reject_class (the seven classes above) and reject_pass (1 or 2). Post texts are not included; they can be retrieved from the tweet_id where the posts are still online (see README). The short quotations in this file are given for documentation, as in the paper.
